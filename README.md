Two lines explaining a traffic jam to a 10-year-old, written for the
[Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run](https://make.fun/c/u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479)
competition on [make.fun](https://make.fun).

## The entry

> A traffic jam is a zipper stuck halfway, with every car a tooth waiting for the one in front to move.
> The zipper fits because cars can only move in order, one after another, but it leaves out that zipper teeth never get impatient, honk, or try to cut in line.

Line 1 is one vivid sentence: a traffic jam as a zipper stuck halfway, each
car a tooth waiting its turn — a thing a 10-year-old can see, hold, and zip.
Line 2 is one honest sentence about the mapping: it names what the zipper
covers (cars move in order, one after another) and what it leaves out
(teeth never get impatient, honk, or cut in line).

## How it works

`main.py` (pure Python standard library, no dependencies, no network) writes
the two lines to `/output/entry.txt` and validates them first: 1–400
characters, exactly two lines, each a single sentence.

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```
